## 1. The eight rules

Each rule carries the one-line test the gate applies.

1. **The first line is a claim or a question a stranger stops for.** No greeting, no brand name.
   *Test:* strip every other line; would a stranger scrolling at 11pm stop on this one? Any "hey,"
   "so," or "xdipx" in line one fails.
2. **The product is on screen by second two and named by second five.** *Test:* the beat sheet
   timestamps the first product frame at 2.0 s or earlier and the spoken noun ("wand," "plug,"
   "lube") at 5.0 s or earlier.
3. **One verifiable fact, with its source class.** Product facts from the PDP spec; brand facts
   from `creative-platform.md` §11, class named as §11 names it. The source varies across the batch
   per platform §5 item 5: a category fact, the label, the box, a question to another cast member,
   Emma answering. "Reviewers in aggregate" is held back until real reviews exist
   (`review_invites` has 0 rows). *Test:* exactly one fact, its class named in the beat sheet,
   checkable against Shopify data or §11.
4. **No character claims to have used, tested, owned, or tried the product.** Wanting is fine.
   Knowing is fine. "I've felt things before" is fine for the cast. Emma never, in any form.
   *Test:* no line puts a speaker's own history with this SKU in play; no Emma line implies she has
   felt anything.
5. **Every frame respects `instagram-campaigns.md` §3.2a on every frame of the clip, and every line
   respects `docs/emma-voice.md`.** Nothing else is gated. *Test:* the frame gate judges the most
   revealing frame, not the first; the voice gate judges the transcript against the charter.
6. **The read-aloud test,** run with the platform's ear test (`creative-platform.md` §5): say the
   line to a friend across a table. *Test:* fail on any of three.
   - **Timid:** your voice drops on the noun.
   - **Porn-copy:** you would be embarrassed for the friend.
   - **Robotic:** the friend could have read it off the box, or a carrier phrase such as "the spec
     sheet says" appears more than once in the batch (owner-notes ledger entry 1).

   A line that fails is cut, not rewritten (ledger entry 1).
7. **Cap 30 s spoken, aim 15 to 25.** Lines of 12 words or fewer, with a breath at each end (the
   InfiniteTalk line-end drift, `video-realism-recipe.md` §6). *Test:* word count at 2.5 words per
   second lands at or under 30 s; no line over 12 words. If it needs more, it is two clips.
8. **The laugh survives.** The pitched laugh reaches the spoken track. *Test:* at least one spoken
   clause is not in the PDP spec and is the pitched laugh; if the laugh exists only in the pitch,
   FAIL robotic.

The close is the sign-off, "Door's open. xdipx." (always "ex-dip-ex"). The spoken CTA follows
`creative-platform.md` §6: "Take a peek" ("I'll take it" on gift clips), half a beat after the
sign-off, on the site-hosted cut and email only, until the owner rules on rented platforms.

## 2. Two hard lines (BLOCK authority)

**2.1 The named-acts fence, exactly as the charter narrows it.** The charter's product-talk
paragraph (`docs/emma-voice.md` v5.6, "Product-talk clips (owner ruling 2026-09-23)"): "the act and
the anatomy are named plainly, and the fence is graphic detail (as the social addendum defines it:
a body narrated mid-act, an arousal state described in the body, crude slang, emoji-anatomy,
solicitation)." Any of the five BLOCKs. The euphemism is a rule 6 fail, not a safety margin.

**2.2 The AI-generated label on every presenter video.** Checked at three points:

- **Post pass** burns it in (the `AI-GENERATED` kicker, `creative-platform.md` §8) and sets the
  platform AI flag where one exists.
- **`script-doctor`** confirms the render brief carries the label instruction before any spend.
- **`social-publish-gate`** confirms the label is on the row before anything posts.

Per platform:

- **Instagram:** platform AI flag set plus the burned-in kicker.
- **X:** no flag exists, the burned-in kicker is the only disclosure and its absence BLOCKs.

A missing label at any point BLOCKs.

## 3. The read-aloud gate, operationally

One pass, read aloud at 2.5 to 3 words per second. Fail the line on any of:

- more than one parse pause per sentence (you had to re-read to know where the stress goes);
- running long at natural pace (the clip exceeds 30 s at 2.5 wps);
- any phrase only ever seen in marketing copy;
- a hesitation at a euphemism.

§3 is the mechanical layer under rule 6; a §3 trip is reported as the rule 6 mode it produced.

**Split of labor.** `script-doctor` owns pacing, parse, and length (the first two conditions).
`emma-empathy-reviewer` owns the register defects (a marketing-only phrase, a hesitation at a
euphemism) plus the hard lines (§2) and shame tone. Both run the batch-level carrier-phrase check
(rule 6). Identical bar: both agents run all four fail conditions and file independently; neither
reads the other's verdict before filing.

## 4. The seven formats

One example line each, from `creative-platform.md` §7. Rotate across the week.

1. **"The spec nobody reads."** One number or feature from the spec sheet, why it matters, done.
   Jade: "Tapered for the prostate. Flared base, so it stays where you put it."
2. **"Ask Emma."** A cast member asks the shy question; Emma answers in one breath.
   Emma: "Worn at the base during sex. You both feel it."
3. **"Two on the counter."** Same category, two products, the one-sentence difference.
   Marcus: "Wand for the whole body. Bullet for one job. Pick your afternoon." (Not from the
   platform's twelve; facts checked against Shopify data before shipping.)
4. **"Gift math."** Who it is for, what it says about the giver, what it costs.
   Marcus: "He wears it, you feel it. Finally, he accessorizes."
5. **"Material class."** Silicone versus glass versus steel, or lube chemistry, in 20 seconds.
   Diego: "It's glass, so warm it or chill it. Your call, gorgeous."
6. **"Vivian's verdict."** The mid-50s confidante says the unshockable thing.
   Vivian: "Every man has a prostate. Fewer have said hello."
7. **"Sofia dares you."** Names the want directly; the product is the answer.
   Sofia: "Air on the clitoris, zero effort from me. I want that."

## 5. Banned phrases

The platform's ten, by reference (`creative-platform.md` §7, where the replacements live):

1. "Personal massager," "adult toy"
2. "Down there," "your special place"
3. "Intimate moments," "me time"
4. "Spice things up"
5. "Naughty," "cheeky," "guilty pleasure"
6. "Game-changer," "next level"
7. "Nobody will ever know"
8. "Trust me," "you'll thank me later"
9. "If you know, you know," or a wink for the word
10. "Grab yours," "shop now," "link in bio" aloud

Any one fails rule 6 (robotic or timid) on sight.

## 6. What a pitch carries

`series-showrunner` files each clip with these, keyed in `scriptJson.pitch`:

- **Product,** from the merchandising shortlist on the brief only (`productHandle`).
- **Format,** from §4 (`format`).
- **Production mode** (`mode`): `talking` or `voiceover`, chosen by episode-writer with
  series-showrunner, stated with one reason (a demonstration or a product-forward frame favours
  voiceover; a line that needs a face favours talking).
- **Speaker** (`speaker`), and the **listener** if any, silent, on camera (`listener`).
- **The one fact** (`fact`), with its source class per rule 3 (`factSource`).
- **The one laugh:** the speaker's personality doing something with that fact, never at the
  customer or a safety fact (`laugh`).
- **First-frame concept,** per `video-realism-recipe.md`, not restated here (`firstFrameConcept`).
- **Estimated cost** (`estCostUsd`).
- **The ElevenLabs read,** on the speaker's cast voice id, recipe §7 (`readAudioUrl`). Phase 2:
  until it ships, "missing" is reported, not failed.
- **Slot or alternate** (`alternate`).
