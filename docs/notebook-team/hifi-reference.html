How Do You Choose Your First Sex Toy?

A plain place to start, no experience assumed.

E

Guides · 6 min read · Last reviewed Jul 2026
Post hero · editorial, warm, product-in-context · LCP · static · never wrapped

Buying your first one can feel like a confession. It is actually just shopping. The trick is to stop asking which toy is best and start asking what you are curious about, because the honest answer to that question narrows the whole catalog down fast.

There are only a few things worth deciding up front: what kind of sensation you want to explore, whether you want something that touches you or works a little differently, and how much you want to spend. Everything else is detail you can learn as you go.

What kind of sensation are you curious about?

Most first toys fall into a few families. Vibration is the broadest and the most forgiving to start with. Air pulse is different: it does not touch you, it pulses the air around one spot. That is the trick, and it is the reason people describe it as a completely separate feeling.

If you are not sure, start broad. A simple, well-reviewed vibrator teaches you more about your own preferences than any guide can, and it stays useful long after you have figured them out.

How much should you spend on a first toy?

Enough to get body-safe materials and a motor that lasts, and no more than that. The materials worth paying for have names: medical-grade silicone, glass, stainless steel. Those are named on every product page, so you never have to guess.

Common questions

Is a beginner toy waterproof?

Many are, and the spec is listed on each product page. Waterproof toys are also the easiest to clean, which is a real advantage for a first one.

Do you need lube with your first toy?

It usually makes things more comfortable. Water-based lube works with every material, so it is the safe default to start with.
